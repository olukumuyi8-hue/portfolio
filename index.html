Text classification and sentiment tagging

Structured labelling against a written guideline

Objective

Assign each text item to the correct category, sentiment and intent so the dataset is consistent enough to train or test a model.

Inputs and outputs

Input: short and long text items with a label schema. Output: one validated label set per item, plus a note where an item is ambiguous.

Method

  • Read the guideline and its edge-case examples before labelling the first item.
  • Label by what the text says, not by what I assume the author meant.
  • Flag mixed or unclear items instead of forcing a label.
  • Re-check a sample of my own labels against the guideline before submitting.

Quality controls

Consistency across similar items, guideline compliance, and acceptance rate on review.

Tools

Annotation platformsExcelGoogle Workspace

Named entity recognition (NER)

Finding and typing the entities inside text

Objective

Mark people, organisations, dates, places and other entity types so extraction models learn clean boundaries.

Inputs and outputs

Input: raw text and an entity type list. Output: spans tagged with the right type and no overlapping or partial boundaries.

Method

  • Apply the guideline’s rules on what counts as one entity, including titles, abbreviations and nested names.
  • Tag the full span, not just the keyword.
  • Resolve conflicts by the guideline’s priority order and record the reasoning for unusual cases.

Quality controls

Boundary accuracy, correct type, and no missed entities in a second read.

Annotation platformsData management systems

Pairwise comparison and human feedback (RLHF)

Judging which model response is better, and why

Objective

Compare model-generated responses against a rubric and give preference signals that help improve model behaviour.

Inputs and outputs

Input: a prompt with two or more responses. Output: a preference ranking, rubric scores and a short written justification.

Method

  • Check each response for accuracy, relevance, tone and guideline compliance.
  • Verify factual claims before scoring them.
  • Write the justification in plain, evidence-based sentences that point to the specific problem or strength.
  • Stay consistent as rubrics and guidelines evolve.

Quality controls

Reasoned judgements, fact-checking and consistent rubric use across tasks.

Tools

Crowdsourcing platformsRating rubrics

Search relevance and query evaluation

Rating how well results answer what a person asked

Objective

Rate how well a result satisfies a query so search systems can be measured and improved.

Inputs and outputs

Input: a query and one or more results. Output: a relevance rating, with a note on the reason for low scores.

Method

  • Work out the likely intent behind the query first.
  • Open and verify the result rather than rating by title alone.
  • Apply the rating scale as written and avoid drifting toward the middle.

Quality controls

Intent accuracy, verified results and scale consistency.

Audio and video transcription

Verbatim and clean-read formats

Objective

Turn recordings into accurate, correctly formatted transcripts that meet turnaround deadlines.

Inputs and outputs

Input: audio or video files and a style guide. Output: a verbatim or clean-read transcript, with speaker labels where required.

Method

  • Follow the style guide on fillers, false starts and unclear speech.
  • Review the full transcript against the recording before delivery.
  • Use the same approach for subtitle and caption review and transcript quality checking.

Quality controls

Accuracy, format compliance and on-time delivery.

Content moderation and dataset quality review

Reviewing content and data against policy

Objective

Check content and datasets against policy and quality rules, and catch errors before they reach a model or a customer.

Inputs and outputs

Input: content items or dataset samples plus a policy or checklist. Output: pass or fail decisions with a note for each failure.

Method

  • Check for missing information, inconsistencies and guideline violations.
  • Keep decisions consistent by following the written policy each time.
  • Report recurring problems so the guideline or process can be improved. This draws on my earlier work in quality monitoring and process improvement, which cut average case handling time by 18% at Anne Arundel County Government.

Quality controls

Decision consistency and clear written reasoning.
